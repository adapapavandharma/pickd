The three noise-cancelling headphones worth buying

One is the best. One is the sensible one. One is for people who have already decided they live inside Apple. Everything else in this category is noise.

Updated 3 September 2026 · 3 picks

Noise cancelling is the rare feature where the marketing and the experience actually match. The first time you put on a good pair on a plane and the engine drone drops away, it stops being a spec and becomes the reason you own them.

The catch is that the category is enormous and almost entirely undifferentiated. There are perhaps four headphones that matter and about two hundred that exist. This is the short list, and each entry says who it is wrong for as well as who it is right for.

The picks, in one line each

Best overall
Sony WH-1000XM5 The quietest, and it is not especially close. Best value
Soundcore Life Q30 Roughly 80% of the Sony for a fraction of the money. Best if you have an iPhone
AirPods Pro (2nd generation) The transparency mode is the actual product.
Best overall

Sony WH-1000XM5

★★★★★★★★★★4.6 · 28,431 ratings
$348.00 Check price Full write-up →

The XM5 is the one to buy if the cancellation itself is what you are paying for. On low-frequency drone — aircraft, trains, the hum of an office HVAC — nothing else at any price reliably beats it. Sony finally fixed the call quality too, which was the standing embarrassment of the XM4.

Thirty hours of battery means charging becomes something you do occasionally rather than something you think about. Multipoint means your laptop and phone are both connected and it hands over without you touching anything.

Best for
Anyone who flies, commutes, or works in a room with other people in it.
Skip it if
You live out of a small bag. These no longer fold flat and the case is genuinely bulky — the XM4 packed better.
Best value

Soundcore Life Q30

★★★★★★★★★★4.5 · 78,320 ratings
$59.99 Check price Full write-up →

This is the honest answer for most people. The cancellation is good rather than exceptional — it takes the edge off a train carriage rather than erasing it — but the gap is much smaller than the price gap.

The battery is absurd. Forty hours with cancellation on means you will forget what the charger looks like. And there is a real freedom in owning headphones you would be annoyed rather than devastated to lose.

Best for
Anyone who wants the feature without the flagship price, and anyone who loses things.
Skip it if
You care about calls. The microphones are adequate and no better, and the default tuning is bass-heavy until you go into the app and fix it.
Best if you have an iPhone

AirPods Pro (2nd generation)

★★★★★★★★★★4.7 · 54,219 ratings
$189.99 Check price Full write-up →

Buying these for the noise cancelling slightly misses the point. The reason to own them is transparency mode, which sounds so natural that you forget you are wearing anything — you can hold a conversation, order a coffee, cross a road. Nothing else this small does it as convincingly.

Inside the Apple ecosystem the switching between devices is effortless in a way that is hard to appreciate until you have it. Outside it, you are paying a large premium for features you cannot use.

Best for
iPhone owners who want something pocketable and hate over-ear headphones.
Skip it if
You use Android. Genuinely — buy something else, you lose most of what you are paying for.

The short version

If you want one sentence: buy the Soundcore unless you fly often, in which case buy the Sony, unless you own an iPhone and hate over-ear headphones, in which case buy the AirPods.

What none of these will do is fix a bad listening environment permanently. Noise cancelling works best on constant low-frequency sound and much less well on speech — an open-plan office full of talking is the hardest case for all three, and no headphone solves it completely.

Questions people actually ask

Does noise cancelling damage your hearing?

The opposite, generally. The mechanism is destructive interference, not volume — and because you are not fighting background noise, most people listen at lower volumes with cancellation on than without it.

Is the difference between good and great cancellation actually noticeable?

On a plane, yes, immediately. In a quiet room, barely. Buy for the environment you will actually use them in rather than for the specification.
